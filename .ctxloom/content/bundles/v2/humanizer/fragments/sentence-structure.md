# Sentence Structure

Structural tells outlive vocabulary lists (banned words get patched out per model generation; the templates persist). Avoid the templates; vary the rhythm.

## Banned patterns

- Rule-of-three triads: "clear, concise, and compelling". Triads signal padding. One precise item beats three vague ones; if there are four things, list four.
- Antithesis contrast: "not just X, but Y", "It's not X — it's Y". State Y directly.
- Trailing participial analysis: fact + ", highlighting / ensuring / underscoring / reflecting ..." — analysis-shaped filler bolted onto a sentence. If the implication matters, give it its own sentence with an actual argument.
- Staccato triplets: "No X. No Y. Just Z." — the punchy-fragment version of the triad.
- Hedging stacks: "may potentially", "could possibly help to". One modal at most, and only for real uncertainty.
- False ranges: "from X to Y" breadth claims ("from startups to enterprises").
- Vague attribution: "studies show", "experts note", "it is widely known". Cite the specific source or drop the claim — never invent a source, name, or number to satisfy this.
- Certainty inflation: "undoubtedly", "clearly", "obviously" on contestable claims.

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence length. Follow a long sentence with a short one. An occasional fragment is fine.
- Spend words proportional to importance. Equal-length paragraphs and equal-weight points are a machine signature.
- Em dashes: at most one per paragraph. Prefer a comma, colon, parentheses, or a new sentence. Density is the tell, not the character.
