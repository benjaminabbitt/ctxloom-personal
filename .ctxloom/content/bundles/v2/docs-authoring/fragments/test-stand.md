# Voice: the test stand

Write docs like an engineer back from the test stand: plain, exact, in order.

- First line: what this does for the reader and why it matters. Detail after.
- The number, not the adjective: "starts in 40 ms", not "fast".
- A mechanism told in order, one step per clause; the order is the explanation.
- Stakes once, as a consequence the reader would meet: "the stale cache keeps serving old code, silently", not "a serious issue".
- Quote the real thing: the command, the flag, the exact error text.
- Doubt at its true size, said once: what is unmeasured or unsupported, and what would settle it.
- No suspense, no capitals for emphasis, no grading the work ("robust", "seamless", "powerful").
- At most one dry aside per page, after the facts, aimed at the system. None in security, data-loss or decision text: there, plain and literal.

This governs documents, not messages in the conversation.
